How it works

Each team's goals are projected separately for 5-on-5 (expected goals + actual goals for/against per 60) and the power play (PP and PK efficiency × penalties drawn and taken), plus other situations. The opposing starting goalie's goals saved above expected scales goals allowed, back-to-backs cut the tired team's scoring ~8% (opponent +6.5%), and team skating speed nudges fast teams up (+2% per step) and their opponents down (−1.7%). Data from the 2020-21 season onward only (MoneyPuck, NHL API, DailyFaceoff, Action Network). Runs on GitHub Actions every 2 hours from 11 AM to 9 PM ET.

Projected goals bar
Each team's projected goals on a 0–4.5 scale. The tick marks the high-scoring cutoff; a solid bar means past it.
P(7+)
Model's chance of 7+ total goals: wins an over 6.5 and an over 6 (exactly 6 pushes an over 6). NHL average ≈ 45%.
OVER FLAG
Both teams past the cutoff and the line is 6 or 6.5. These are the picks that count in the record.
Goalie badges
✓ Confirmed / Likely / Projected starter, from DailyFaceoff, refreshed every run (every 2 hours). "Changed" = different from the starter the projection used.
B2B
Team played yesterday: its projected scoring is cut ~8% and its opponent's raised ~6.5%.
Injuries & lineups
ESPN's injury list plus DailyFaceoff's projected lineups checked against the official NHL roster. Players who are out, on IR, suspended, or missing from tonight's lineup (scratch) are replaced by a replacement-level skater. GF = change to his team's scoring, GA = change to goals against (shown for whichever is bigger; hover for both). Day-to-day players are shown but not adjusted.
Breakdown
Each team's goals = (5-on-5 + power play + other situations) × opposing-goalie × back-to-back × team speed × injury adjustments. A + goalie number means the opposing goalie has been below average.
Team speed (Spd)
How often a team's skaters hit 20+ mph (NHL EDGE), last season vs the league. Fast teams score a little more and allow a little less, so it shifts team projections but barely changes totals.
